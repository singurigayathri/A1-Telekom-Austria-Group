    Method and limits

    • Financials: FY2025 annual figures from stockanalysis.com (retrieved 3 Nov 2026). Revenue growth is FY2025 vs FY2024.
    • EBITDA = EBIT + depreciation & amortisation from the cash-flow statement. This is reported IFRS, not company-adjusted, so one-offs such as impairments move it. Orange and Telefónica EBIT fell sharply in 2025: check the annual report before quoting their margins.
    • Swisscom revenue jumped in 2025 because of the Vodafone Italia acquisition, so its growth and debt are not like-for-like.
    • A1 cross-check: A1's own FY2025 report gives revenue EUR 5,577m, EBITDA EUR 2,062m (37.0%) and capex EUR 889m including spectrum. The peer table uses stockanalysis.com for every company (A1: revenue 5,474, derived EBITDA 1,987, margin 36.3%), so all companies share one source and definition. A1's net debt excluding leases is about EUR 392m (financial debt 754 less cash 362); the 1,848 in the table includes leases, which is closer to how the other telcos report.
    • Net debt is total debt less cash and investments as shown by the source. Definitions differ by company (leases, hybrids).
    • Prices (automatic): a free GitHub job runs every 30 minutes on weekdays while European markets are open, downloads one year of daily closes from Yahoo Finance for each company's home-exchange ticker (local currency, adjusted for dividends and splits), and saves them as data/benchmark_prices.json. The page reads that file when it opens, so it always shows the latest update (about 15 minutes delayed during the day, official close after the market shuts) with no key and no clicking. Benchmark is the STOXX Europe 600 index (^STOXX). Each company's returns are in its own currency, so there is no EUR/USD noise.
    • Prices (optional manual refresh): you can still paste a Twelve Data key on the Market tab to pull US-listed ADR prices (USD). This replaces the daily data until you reload the page.
    • Volatility = standard deviation of daily log returns × √252. Beta = covariance with the benchmark ÷ variance of the benchmark on matching dates. Max drawdown = worst peak-to-trough fall in the window.
    • Not yet covered: share prices for Hrvatski Telekom, Telekom Slovenije and Makedonski Telekom (no free price feed), A1's own country KPIs, Telekom Slovenije financials, GARCH.

    Thesis method (Thesis test tab)

    • Research question: do telco fundamentals (EBITDA margin, capex intensity, leverage) explain differences in share-price volatility across European telcos, with A1 as the case company?
    • Data: up to 10 years of daily prices and annual financial statements from Yahoo Finance (via yfinance), downloaded by a weekly GitHub job. Companies Yahoo does not return are listed on the tab. Yahoo normally gives only about 4 years of annual statements, so the panel covers roughly the last 4 fiscal years.
    • Panel: one row per company and fiscal year. Volatility = standard deviation of daily log returns over the 12 months ending at the fiscal year-end × √252. EBITDA = EBIT + depreciation & amortisation. Rows with non-positive EBITDA or extreme leverage are dropped.
    • Regressions (OLS): volatility on EBITDA margin, capex/sales and net debt/EBITDA. M1 pooled; M2 adds year effects; M3 adds company effects; M4 adds market beta. Standard errors are clustered by company. Stars: *** p<0.01, ** p<0.05, * p<0.10.
    • Robustness: total-return volatility (dividends included), EUR-converted returns, fundamentals lagged one year, without A1, and two sub-periods.
    • GARCH(1,1): fitted per company on the last 5 years of daily price returns (normal errors). Persistence = α + β. Long-run volatility = √(ω / (1 − α − β)) × √252.
    • Event study: market model fitted on 250 trading days that end 30 days before the event, benchmark STOXX Europe 600, total-return series. Abnormal return = actual minus predicted. CAR = sum over the window; t-statistic uses the estimation-window residual deviation. Events come from events.csv plus earnings dates where Yahoo provides them. Check that Yahoo adjusted the price around the 22 Sep 2023 EuroTeleSites spin-off.
    • Limits: few companies and years; volatility and fundamentals are measured over the same year (the lagged model addresses reverse causality only partly); reported IFRS figures include one-offs; Yahoo data is free but unaudited; no sentiment (AI) layer yet.
    • Reproducibility: every number on the tab comes from data/thesis_results.json and data/panel.csv, both downloadable, with the update date shown.
